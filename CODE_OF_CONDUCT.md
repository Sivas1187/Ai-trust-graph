# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

AI Trust Graph exists to make AI systems more trustworthy through rigorous, evidence-based methodology. We hold ourselves to the same standard of intellectual honesty in how we treat each other that we ask assessors to apply to evidence: precise, fair, and willing to be corrected.

This project welcomes contributors regardless of experience level, background, employer, or nationality. Participation is open under the rules in [Artifact #11 — Governance & Certification Model](docs/11-governance-and-certification-model.md) §8 ("Community and Extension Governance"), which this document implements.

## Expected behavior

- **Be evidence-led.** Challenge ideas, methods, and conclusions on their merits, with reasoning or evidence, the same way this methodology asks an assessor to challenge a control result.
- **Be precise.** Say what you mean. If you're proposing a change to canonical meaning, say so explicitly rather than let it hide inside a "clarification."
- **Disclose conflicts.** If you have a commercial, employment, or other interest relevant to a proposal — including anything connected to ExposureGraph or a competing methodology — say so up front, mirroring the impartiality-disclosure standard the methodology itself requires of assessors (Governance & Certification Model §0.9).
- **Respect confidentiality boundaries.** Never submit client data, employer-confidential material, or content you don't have clear rights to contribute. See [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md).
- **Disagree in good faith.** Reasonable people will disagree about maturity thresholds, control wording, and scoring weights. Say why you disagree; don't just say no.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, personal attacks, or discriminatory language or conduct of any kind.
- Deliberately misrepresenting what a change does in order to get it merged.
- Submitting content that violates the ExposureGraph product boundary (see [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md)) after being told it does.
- Retaliating against someone for raising a good-faith concern, filing an issue, or disagreeing with a maintainer — this mirrors the "no retaliation" protection the methodology requires in its own complaints process (Governance & Certification Model §9.8).
- Sharing another contributor's private information without consent.

## Scope

This code of conduct applies within all project spaces — issues, pull requests, discussions, and any other official communication channel — and in public spaces when someone is representing the project.

## Enforcement

Reports of unacceptable behavior may currently be made directly to the methodology author, Siva Sethumadhavan, until the formal governance bodies described in Artifact #11 (a Governance Council, an Impartiality Committee, an Appeals Panel) are actually constituted — see [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md). All reports will be reviewed and investigated, and will result in a response deemed necessary and appropriate to the circumstances. Confidentiality will be maintained to the extent possible.

Once the governance bodies in Artifact #11 are operational, this project intends to route conduct enforcement through the same complaints, appeals, and sanctions process defined there (§9) — including independent review by someone other than the original decision-maker, and protection against retaliation — rather than maintaining a separate, parallel process. Until then, this document is the operative standard.

## Attribution
